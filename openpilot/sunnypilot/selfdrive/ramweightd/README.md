# ramweightd: weight, air drag and road grade for Ram Heavy Duty trucks

Issue: edfrahm/SP-RamHD-TICI-Port#8 (part of the smoother-cruise epic #2).

`ramweightd` is an on-device service. While the truck is on (onroad), and only on a Ram Heavy Duty (CarParams brand
`chrysler`, platform `RAM_HD_5TH_GEN`), it estimates ten times per second:

- the **combined weight** of truck, people, load and any trailer, and how sure it is (one standard deviation);
- the **air-drag factor** c: drag force in newtons = c times speed squared (speed in meters per second);
- the **road grade** (rise over run, positive uphill), and how sure it is.

It publishes them as the `ramWeightGrade` message, which is logged and uploaded with the drive. **Nothing reads it to
steer, accelerate or brake: in this stage it changes no driving.**

Files:

## Terms

- **Kalman filter**: a standard way to combine noisy measurements with a model of how the estimated values change. It
  keeps a best estimate and its uncertainty, moves the estimate toward each new measurement in proportion to how much
  that measurement is trusted, and narrows the uncertainty only in the directions the measurement actually informs.
- **Innovation**: how far a measurement is from what the filter expected. A measurement more than three standard
  deviations away is rejected as an outlier ("innovation gating").
- **Torque converter**: the fluid coupling between engine and gearbox. Locked, it ties engine speed to wheel speed and
  loses almost no power; slipping, it loses some.

## How the grade is estimated

A two-state Kalman filter: the sine of the grade angle, and the offset of the stability-control sensor's acceleration
reading. Two measurements:

1. **Stability-control sensor** (message `ESP_4`, 0x14C, pending verification in #5): the sensor feels acceleration
   plus gravity along the slope; wheel speed gives the acceleration alone. Their difference is g times the sine of the
   grade (plus the sensor's mounting offset). Both are smoothed by the same filter so their timing matches.
2. **The Comma's own pitch**: openpilot's location filter publishes the device's orientation (`deviceMotion`, called
   `livePose` in older versions) and calibration publishes the device's mounting angles (`extrinsicsCalibration`, called
   `liveCalibration` before). Turned into the truck's frame the same way controlsd does, this gives the truck's pitch.

With both, the Comma's pitch fixes the sensor's slow offset and the sensor follows quick changes. Today the sensor is
not in the database, so the **Comma's pitch is used alone**, with a wider uncertainty: never below 0.015, and growing
with the grade it reads (0.6 of it). See "Findings from the first real drives" for why.

The body pitches nose-up when the truck speeds up, which tilts both sensors; `BODY_PITCH_PER_ACCEL` (radians per
m/s²) corrects for it. It is 0 until `calibrate.py` fits it.

## How the weight and air drag are estimated

Driving force from engine power, which works in any gear without knowing the gear ratios:

    force = efficiency × engine torque × engine angular speed / truck speed

minus the power spent spinning up the engine itself (measured from the engine speed change). The measurement equation:

    force = (mass + rotating parts) × acceleration + mass × g × (sine(grade) + rolling resistance × cosine(grade)) + c × speed²

A two-state Kalman filter fits mass and c together. Both sides are averaged over the same one-second window of clean
samples (ten samples), so their timing matches exactly, and the acceleration over the window is the speed change
divided by the window length. Windows do not overlap.

- **Uncertainty narrows only with varied driving.** Steady cruising on a level road only tells the filter
  mass × g × rolling resistance + c × speed² (one combination): such windows are used only for that combination, and the
  weight stays uncertain. Accelerations (and, with the stability sensor, grades) separate weight from drag, and different
  speeds separate rolling resistance from drag. With the Comma's pitch alone, only accelerations count, because those
  grades are too uncertain. (Without this rule, noise in the measured grade and acceleration would look like information
  and pull the weight down: the "errors in the input variables" bias.)
- **Outliers** are rejected by innovation gating (three standard deviations).
- **Weight changes only while stopped**: no process noise on the mass while moving. The drag factor may drift slowly
  (wind, direction).

Starting values ("priors"), all at the top of `estimator.py`:

| constant | value | why |
|---|---|---|
| `EFFICIENCY` | 0.85 | engine power reaching the tires with the converter locked: gearbox about 0.92-0.95, axles 0.95-0.97, minus fan, alternator, pumps |
| `ROLLING_RESISTANCE` | 0.009 | light-truck tires (load range E) on asphalt: 0.008-0.012 |
| `DRAG_PRIOR` | 0.90 ± 0.35 N/(m/s)² | 0.5 × 1.2 kg/m³ × 0.409 (Ram's published drag coefficient, 2019 Ram HD press kit) × 3.67 m² (an **unofficial** frontal area: 83.5 in wide × 80.2 in high × 0.85). Wide because the area is a guess; a trailer can add 1 to 3. |
| `ENGINE_INERTIA_KGM2` | 1.5 | engine, flexplate and locked converter; matters in low gears |
| `ROTATING_MASS_KG` | 120 | wheels, tires, rotors, shafts act like this much extra mass when speeding up |
| `BODY_PITCH_PER_ACCEL` | 0 | fitted by `calibrate.py` |
| `PITCH_GRADE_SCALE` | 1.0 | multiplies the Comma's pitch grade; see the findings below |

## Clean moments

Only clean moments are used for the weight (`clean_reasons` in `estimator.py`; the reasons are published in
`notCleanReasons`):

- speed above 5 m/s, a forward gear;
- torque converter locked;
- no service brake (brake pedal switch `ESP_1 Brake_Pedal_State`, or brake pressure `ESP_8 BRK_PRESSURE` above 20 in its
  raw units; its resting value is still to be checked);
- no exhaust brake, no shift in progress;
- engine pulling: engine torque above 60 Nm and either the accelerator pressed or the factory cruise asking for torque
  (`DAS_3 ENGINE_TORQUE_REQUEST`, bus 2);
- no traction control, no sharp turn (sideways acceleration above 1.5 m/s² from the Comma's yaw rate), a known grade.

Until the transmission signals are verified (#5):

- **Converter-lock fallback (labeled, published in `converterLockRule`)**: the converter counts as locked when engine
  speed divided by rear-wheel speed holds steady (every sample within 1 percent of the average) for 2 seconds. In a
  fixed gear with the converter locked this ratio is the gear ratio times the axle ratio. A converter slipping by a
  steady amount at light load can pass this test; the real signal will replace it. The fallback's result is published
  in `converterLocked` but **not listed in `availableSignals`** (it is not a decoded signal).
- **Shift in progress**: a shift changes the ratio, so the steady-ratio rule excludes it.
- **Exhaust brake**: it works only with the accelerator released, so "engine pulling" already excludes it.

## Stops, persistence and starting each drive

A **qualifying stop** (the load may have changed): standing still with the shifter in park, or a door opening, or a
verified trailer signal changing, or standing still for 120 seconds. 60 seconds would be too short: long red lights
would reset the learned weight several times a drive.

At a qualifying stop the weight is **kept** but its uncertainty **widens** so that a hitched trailer up to the 40,000 lb
design ceiling can be learned: the ceiling sits two standard deviations above the current weight, and the drag
uncertainty grows to 1.0. (Once a trailer signal is verified and says "no trailer", only a payload-sized widening,
1,500 kg, is used.) **Every drive starts like a qualifying stop.**

The estimate is kept in the setting `RamWeightGradeState` at qualifying stops, every minute, and when the drive ends.
The kept uncertainty is the learned one, not a widening nothing was learned from since. The next drive starts from it
(widened), but only when it belongs to the same platform and configuration. No VIN, no location is stored.

## Fuel

With a fuel-level signal (not found yet, #5): fuel burned = level change × tank size × fuel weight. The level sloshes,
so while moving it is smoothed over 5 minutes (the first 5 minutes are averaged, so a sloshing first reading does not
linger), and only decreases count (the level estimate only moves down, and only when the smoothed level is more than
0.5 percent below it). At a stop the level is calm: after 20 seconds, once it has held steady for 10 seconds, a rise of
at least 5 percent is refueling, added to the weight at once. `fuelBurnedKg` is the fuel burned this drive.

Fuel weights: diesel 7.079 lb per US gallon, gasoline 6.25 (US Department of Energy, Transportation Energy Data Book,
edition 40, table 6.17: diesel 7.079; gasoline 6.0-6.5, 6.25 is the middle).

## Starting weight

Published in `startMassKg`, `startMassStdKg` and `startSource`. In order:

1. **Setting** (`setting`): `CruiseBaseWeightLb` when above 0 (Eric's truck: 8,400 lb with a full tank), minus the
   missing fuel.
2. **Published table** (`table`): Ram's published curb weight for the identified configuration
   + **529 lb options allowance** + **250 lb** (driver and everyday gear) − missing fuel.
   - Options allowance = 8,040 lb (Eric's truck empty, full tank) − 7,511 lb (Ram's published curb weight for Eric's
     configuration: Stellantis 2022 Ram 2500 4x4 weight and tow chart, body builder instruction of 07/07/2021, page 5,
     table "RAM 2500 - 4X4 - CREW CAB - ST - 6'4'' STANDARD BOX"; the base Tradesman's weight, full fuel tank). The same
     529 lb is used for every configuration, RamBox included (Eric's decision).
   - The VIN cannot tell a **RamBox** from the standard box: the standard box's weight is used, and the RamBox's extra
     weight (129 lb on Eric's configuration) widens the uncertainty.
   - On a **3500 with the Cummins** the VIN cannot tell the standard output (68RFE gearbox) from the high output
     (AS69RC): the engine part number decides when the table knows it, otherwise the two are averaged with a wider
     uncertainty.
   - **Bed average** (`tableBedAverage`): the VIN tells the bed (Regular Cab always 8 ft, Mega Cab always 6 ft 4 in,
     Crew Cab codes say which), so this is only for VIN codes missing from the table: the average of both beds, wider.
   - Eric's configuration (2022 Ram 2500 Crew Cab 6 ft 4 in, 4x4, Cummins standard output, 68RFE): 7,511 + 529 + 250
     = **8,290 lb with a full 31-gallon tank**.
3. **Fallback** (`fallback`): the platform's weight from CarParams (3,541 kg, 7,807 lb) with a wide uncertainty
   (600 kg), when the configuration or its weight is unknown.

When the fuel level is not available, the tank is assumed half full, and the uncertainty includes not knowing it.

**Fuel tank size** (Eric's decision): if the truck reports its tank size (a hook is in the mapping table,
`fuel_tank_gal`; not found yet), it is used. Otherwise the table's tank is used, except that **Crew Cab trucks with the
8 ft box of model years 2020-2024 are assumed to have the optional 50-gallon tank** (standard there: 32 gallons; 31 on
6 ft 4 in boxes; 28 on a Regular Cab diesel; owner's manuals' fluid capacities for 2020-2023 and the 2024 media fact
sheet, as listed in the research table). Reason: the optional tank is common on those trucks and the VIN cannot tell.
Then the tank capacity is 50 gallons for the fuel calculations, the extra full-tank fuel (18 gallons × 7.079 lb for
diesel, × 6.25 for gasoline) is added to the published curb weight (which includes a full standard tank), and the
starting uncertainty grows by half that fuel's weight (the chance it is really the 32-gallon tank) and by 30 lb for
the larger tank's own weight, which is not published.

### Configuration from the VIN and part numbers

Only the table's rules are used: no VIN position is known in the code except the model-year character at position 10
(`model_year_codes`). Positions 5-7 are one three-character code (series, rear wheels, drive, cab, bed, and the
Power Wagon / Rebel package); position 8 is the engine ("L" = Cummins of either output). Engine module part numbers
(`CarParams.carFw`, address 0x7E0) add what the table knows (`52370231` = Cummins standard output, 68RFE, 2500: Eric's
truck reports `52370231AK`). The VIN check digit is verified. **The VIN is never logged, stored or published**; the
configuration is published in words in `configuration`.

### The published-weight table

`ram_hd_weights.json` is generated by `make_weight_table.py` from the full research table (about 1 MB, not committed;
retrieved 2026-10-16). That table was built from Stellantis documents: the Ram Heavy Duty weight-and-tow charts and body
builder instructions (curb weight = the published base weight of the base trim with a full fuel tank), owner's
manuals (fuel tanks), media specifications, the 2019 press kit (drag coefficient) and the NHTSA VIN decoding guides.
The slim table keeps per configuration: model year, series, rear wheels, cab, bed, bed length, box variant, package,
drive, engine, transmission, recommended curb weight, its kind, standard and optional fuel tank, and the source id; plus
the sources, fuel weights, VIN rules and engine part numbers. To regenerate:

    python openpilot/sunnypilot/selfdrive/ramweightd/make_weight_table.py <path>/ram_hd_specs.json

## Settings

In **Settings > Vehicle** (the Chrysler/Ram panel), shown only on a Ram Heavy Duty:

- **Starting Weight** (`CruiseBaseWeightLb`): EDIT, type pounds (0 to 40,000). 0 uses Ram's published weight.
- **Known Extra Load** (`CruiseKnownExtraLb`): 0 to 2,000 lb in 10 lb steps. For a validation drive: the known added
  load (the 250 lb tank, passengers of known weight). **The estimator does not use it** (that would hide its error); it
  is only published as `knownExtraKg`, so that accuracy = estimate − (starting weight + known extra) can be measured.
  Set it back to 0 afterwards.
- **Weight Estimate**: read-only, the kept estimate and its uncertainty in pounds.

sunnylink's settings schema (`openpilot/sunnypilot/sunnylink/settings_ui.json`) does not need entries for these: its
tests check only the settings it lists, and nothing requires every backed-up setting to be listed.

## Signals: what is verified, what is pending (#5)

All in `RAM_SIGNALS` in `ram_signals.py` (message, signal, bus, status). Known today: `ECM_1` ENGINE_RPM and
ENGINE_TORQUE, `ESP_6` wheel speeds, `ESP_8` BRK_PRESSURE and Vehicle_Speed, `ESP_1` Brake_Pedal_State, `ECM_5`
Accelerator_Position, `DAS_3` ACC_ACTIVE and ENGINE_TORQUE_REQUEST (bus 2); `ESP_2` TCS_ACTIVE is in the shared database
but unchecked on this truck. Pending (named as realfast proposed): `TRANS_SPEED` (0x144) TC_LOCKED, ACTUAL_GEAR,
DESIRED_GEAR, SHIFT_PENDING, INPUT_SPEED, OUTPUT_SPEED; `ESP_4` (0x14C) Acceleration. Not found yet: exhaust brake,
tow/haul, trailer connected, fuel level, fuel tank size.

A signal whose message is not in the database is unavailable and its `ramWeightGrade` field is left out of
`availableSignals`. When #5 adds a verified signal to opendbc, fix its message and signal name in `RAM_SIGNALS` (and
mark it known); nothing else changes. The tests include a copy of the database with the pending signals, so the code
paths that use them are already exercised.

`availableSignals` lists `massKg`, `massStdKg`, `dragNPerMps2` once the filter runs, `grade` and `gradeStd` once it has
a grade measurement, and each decoded truck signal (`engineRpm`, `engineTorqueNm`, `gearActual`, `gearDesired`,
`converterLocked`, `shiftPending`, `transInputRpm`, `transOutputRpm`, `serviceBrakeOn`, `exhaustBrakeOn`, `towHaulOn`,
`trailerConnected`, `fuelLevelPct`) only when it comes from a real signal.

## Replay a recorded drive

    # on the Mac (drives copied to ~/src/ram-hd-c3/rlogs/<route>--<n>/rlog.zst)
    python openpilot/sunnypilot/selfdrive/ramweightd/replay.py 00000779--9043655153 --root ~/src/ram-hd-c3/rlogs --known-mass-lb 8400
    # on the comma 3
    cd /data/openpilot && PYTHONPATH=/data/openpilot /usr/local/venv/bin/python \
      openpilot/sunnypilot/selfdrive/ramweightd/replay.py 00000779--9043655153 --known-mass-lb 8400

It runs exactly the on-device code over the drive and prints the estimate every minute, the qualifying stops, why
moments were not clean, which grade source and converter rule were used, the estimate kept for the next drive and,
with `--known-mass-lb`, the accuracy. Options: `--base-weight-lb` (as the setting), `--csv FILE` (10 Hz time series),
`--dbc` (another message database), `--efficiency`, `--rolling-resistance`, `--body-pitch`, `--pitch-grade-scale` (try
constants). The device settings are not read; the VIN is never printed.

## Calibrate the constants

From drives with a **confirmed** combined weight (truck, people, load, fuel at the start; no refueling during a
drive), with varied speeds and grades, strong accelerations and long climbs:

    python openpilot/sunnypilot/selfdrive/ramweightd/calibrate.py <route> [<route> ...] --known-mass-lb 8400 [--root DIR]

It fits efficiency, rolling resistance and the body pitch (which also absorbs rotating-mass errors), plus the air drag
for information, and prints the values to put at the top of `estimator.py`. It warns when the drives do not separate
them or the values are not plausible. `--fit-grade-scale` instead checks the grade: how much of the measured grade the
engine force follows (1.00 when the grade measurement is right). Check new constants with `replay.py` on other drives.

## Findings from the first real drives (2026-10-16)

Three drives (00000776, 00000778, 00000779; 49 minutes), replayed read-only with only today's verified signals, so the
Comma's pitch alone and the converter-lock fallback. **Eric's weight on those drives is not confirmed** (8,400 lb is
likely: Eric alone with his standard gear). No GPS fix was recorded, so the grade could not be checked against
altitude.

- The Comma's calibrated pitch (identical to what controlsd logs) disagrees with the grade implied by the engine force:
  the force follows only about a third of the grade the pitch reads (`calibrate.py --fit-grade-scale`: 0.33). With
  the unconfirmed 8,400 lb, everything else comes out at textbook values (efficiency 0.83, rolling resistance 0.009,
  air drag 0.88). The ratio of the grade and acceleration effects (about 0.35-0.44 of the physical value) does not depend
  on the weight or the efficiency. It is not a selection effect: it holds with the converter in any state, cruise on or
  off. The cause is not known yet.
- So without the stability sensor the grade's uncertainty is wide and grows with the grade, and only accelerations
  inform the weight. With the shipped constants the kept estimates were 8,019 ± 1,588, 7,665 ± 1,460 and
  6,148 ± 969 lb. With `--pitch-grade-scale 0.33` they were 8,093 ± 1,080, 8,079 ± 1,174 and 7,073 ± 676 lb.
- To settle it: verify `ESP_4` (#5) so the stability sensor measures the grade; record a drive with a GPS fix on a road
  with known grades; confirm the weight on a scale and run `calibrate.py`.
