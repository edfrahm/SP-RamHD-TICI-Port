# ramweightd: weight, air drag and road grade for Ram Heavy Duty trucks

Issue: edfrahm/SP-RamHD-TICI-Port#8 (part of the smoother-cruise epic #2).

`ramweightd` is an on-device service. While the truck is on (onroad), and only on a Ram Heavy Duty (CarParams brand
`chrysler`, platform `RAM_HD_5TH_GEN`), it estimates ten times per second:

- the **combined weight** of truck, people, load and any trailer, and how sure it is (one standard deviation);
- the **air-drag factor** c: drag force in newtons = c times speed squared (speed in meters per second);
- the **road grade** (rise over run, positive uphill), and how sure it is.

It publishes them as the `ramWeightGrade` message, which is logged and uploaded with the drive. **Nothing reads it to
steer, accelerate or brake: in this stage it changes no driving.**

Files:

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `ramweightd.py` | the service: reads messages and settings, publishes `ramWeightGrade`, keeps the estimate between drives |
| `runner.py` | turns openpilot messages into estimator samples and fills the message; shared with the offline tools |
| `ram_signals.py` | **the one table of every Ram controller area network (CAN) signal the estimator reads** |
| `estimator.py` | the arithmetic: grade filter, weight-and-drag filter, clean-moment rules, stops, fuel |
| `start_weight.py` | the starting weight; the configuration from the vehicle identification number (VIN) and part numbers |

## Terms

- **Kalman filter**: a standard way to combine noisy measurements with a model of how the estimated values change. It
  keeps a best estimate and its uncertainty, moves the estimate toward each new measurement in proportion to how much
  that measurement is trusted, and narrows the uncertainty only in the directions the measurement actually informs.
- **Innovation**: how far a measurement is from what the filter expected. A measurement more than three standard
  deviations away is rejected as an outlier ("innovation gating").
- **Torque converter**: the fluid coupling between engine and gearbox. Locked, it ties engine speed to wheel speed and
  loses almost no power; slipping, it loses some.

## How the grade is estimated

A Kalman filter with three states: the sine of the grade angle, the offset of the stability-control sensor's reading,
and the offset of the Comma's accelerometer. Measurements, in order of preference:

1. **Stability-control sensor** (`ESP_4` Acceleration, 0x14C, verified in #5): it feels acceleration plus gravity
   along the slope; wheel speed gives the acceleration alone. Their difference is g times the sine of the grade (plus a
   small offset; on Eric's drives its grade averaged -0.001 to -0.005, about zero). Both sides are smoothed by the same
   filter so their timing matches.
2. **The Comma's own accelerometer** (the `accelerometer` service, about 104 readings per second, averaged over each
   tenth of a second and turned into the truck's frame with the Comma's mounting angles from `extrinsicsCalibration`):
   the same principle. Compared with ESP_4 it reads 0.13-0.19 m/s² higher (mean 0.16: its bias and what the calibration
   leaves of the mounting tilt) and tilts a further 0.0038 rad nose-up per m/s² of acceleration (the cab pitching on its
   mounts); both are corrected (`COMMA_ACCEL_OFFSET_MPS2`, `COMMA_TILT_PER_ACCEL`). **Used only when ESP_4 is not
   available**: fused with ESP_4, only the two offsets' difference is observable, and the Comma's own offset wanders
   (0.085-0.16 m/s² within Eric's drives); their shared level drifted and read every grade up to 0.009 too steep,
   which made drive 0000076b 700 lb too light (see "Findings").
3. **The Comma's pitch** (`deviceMotion` orientation, called `livePose` in older versions, turned into the truck's
   frame like controlsd does): used **only when no accelerometer is available**, and only when plausible (older
   software logged pitches down to -61°). Then the grade's uncertainty is wide: never below 0.015, growing by 0.6 of
   the grade read.

Why the pitch is a last resort: on Eric's nine drives ESP_4 and the Comma's accelerometer agreed with each other
(correlation 0.88-0.95, slope 0.91-1.06), while the pitch agreed with neither (correlation 0.01-0.80). Its errors
are as large as the real grade changes on mild highway grades, so any fit against it shrinks (regression dilution:
the fitted effect is about var(true) / (var(true) + var(error)), which gave 0.17-0.33 on those drives; the pitch is not
three times too steep: regressed on ESP_4, its slope is 0.93-1.04). See "Findings".

Even ESP_4's grade carries some noise (its 0.08 m/s² steps): the engine force followed about 0.83 of its grade, so the
grade's published uncertainty includes 0.2 of the grade read (`ACCEL_GRADE_REL_ERROR`).

The body pitches nose-up when the truck speeds up, which tilts all three; `BODY_PITCH_PER_ACCEL` (radians per m/s²)
corrects for it. It is 0: the drives did not show a need for it with ESP_4.

## How the weight and air drag are estimated

Driving force from engine power, which works in any gear without knowing the gear ratios:

    force = efficiency × engine torque × engine angular speed / truck speed

minus the power spent spinning up the engine itself (measured from the engine speed change). The measurement equation:

    force = (mass + rotating parts) × acceleration + mass × g × (sine(grade) + rolling resistance × cosine(grade)) + c × speed²

A two-state Kalman filter fits mass and c together. Both sides are averaged over the same **three-second** window of
clean samples, so their timing matches exactly, and the acceleration over the window is the speed change divided by
the window length. Windows do not overlap. (One second was too short: the speed and ESP_4 readings are coarse steps,
and their noise over one second diluted the fit, giving estimates 5-15 percent low with far too small uncertainties.)

- **Only accelerations inform the weight.** A window counts for the weight when its acceleration stands clearly out of
  the noise (three noise widths, including the grade's uncertainty). The grade stays in the equation as a correction
  with its uncertainty. On Eric's drives the acceleration effect matched Eric's weight with the textbook efficiency
  (8,080-8,230 lb at 0.85) while the grade effect read 17 percent low even with ESP_4 (dilution, above).
- **Steady driving** only tells the filter mass × g × rolling resistance + c × speed² (one combination): such windows
  are used only for that combination, and the weight stays uncertain. Different speeds separate rolling resistance from
  drag.
- **Outliers** are rejected by innovation gating (three standard deviations).
- **Weight changes only while stopped**: no process noise on the mass while moving. The drag factor may drift slowly
  (wind, direction).

Starting values ("priors"), all at the top of `estimator.py`:

| constant | value | why |
|---|---|---|
| `EFFICIENCY` | 0.85 | engine power reaching the tires with the converter locked: gearbox about 0.92-0.95, axles 0.95-0.97, minus fan, alternator, pumps. `calibrate.py` on Eric's drives at 8,180-8,400 lb: 0.850-0.872 (six older drives), 0.852 (three new drives) |
| `ROLLING_RESISTANCE` | 0.009 | light-truck tires (load range E) on asphalt: 0.008-0.012. Fitted: 0.0081 (older drives), 0.0088 (new) |
| `DRAG_PRIOR` | 0.90 ± 0.35 N/(m/s)² | 0.5 × 1.2 kg/m³ × 0.409 (Ram's published drag coefficient, 2019 Ram HD press kit) × 3.67 m² (an **unofficial** frontal area: 83.5 in wide × 80.2 in high × 0.85). Wide because the area is a guess; a trailer can add 1 to 3. Fitted on the drives: 0.74-0.78 |
| `ENGINE_INERTIA_KGM2` | 1.5 | engine, flexplate and locked converter; matters in low gears |
| `ROTATING_MASS_KG` | 120 | wheels, tires, rotors, shafts act like this much extra mass when speeding up |
| `BODY_PITCH_PER_ACCEL` | 0 | fitted by `calibrate.py` |
| `COMMA_ACCEL_OFFSET_MPS2`, `COMMA_TILT_PER_ACCEL` | 0.16, 0.0038 | the Comma's accelerometer against ESP_4 on Eric's nine drives (used only without ESP_4) |

## Clean moments

Only clean moments are used for the weight (`clean_reasons` in `estimator.py`; the reasons are published in
`notCleanReasons`):

- speed above 5 m/s, a forward gear;
- torque converter locked;
- no service brake (brake pedal switch `ESP_1 Brake_Pedal_State`, or brake pressure `ESP_8 BRK_PRESSURE` above 20 in its
  raw units; its resting value is still to be checked);
- no exhaust brake, no shift in progress;
- engine pulling: engine torque above 60 Nm and either the accelerator pressed or the factory cruise asking for torque
  (`DAS_3 ENGINE_TORQUE_REQUEST`, bus 2);
- no traction control, no sharp turn (sideways acceleration above 1.5 m/s² from the Comma's yaw rate), a known grade.

Transmission (`TCM_1`, 0x144, verified in #5):

- **Converter locked**: `TC_LOCKED` (engine speed equals the transmission's `INPUT_SPEED` when set).
- **Shift in progress**: there is no shift-pending signal (never set in 549 shifts), so a shift is the desired gear
  (`DESIRED_GEAR`) differing from the actual one (`ACTUAL_GEAR`), or the actual gear having changed within the last
  second. Gear codes 1, 2, 9, 3, 4, 5 are 1st to 6th (published as 1-6); 0 and 11 (stopped, park, reverse) are
  published as 0.
- **Fallback (labeled, published in `converterLockRule`)** when `TCM_1` is not received: the converter counts as
  locked when engine speed divided by rear-wheel speed holds steady (every sample within 1 percent of the average) for
  2 seconds, which also excludes shifts. The fallback's result is published in `converterLocked` but **not listed in
  `availableSignals`** (it is not a decoded signal).
- **Exhaust brake** (not found yet): it works only with the accelerator released, so "engine pulling" excludes it.

## Stops, persistence and starting each drive

A **qualifying stop** (the load may have changed): standing still with the shifter in park, or a door opening, or a
verified trailer signal changing, or standing still for 120 seconds. 60 seconds would be too short: long red lights
would reset the learned weight several times a drive.

At a qualifying stop the weight is **kept** but its uncertainty **widens** so that a hitched trailer up to the 40,000 lb
design ceiling can be learned: the ceiling sits two standard deviations above the current weight, and the drag
uncertainty grows to 1.0. (Once a trailer signal is verified and says "no trailer", only a payload-sized widening,
1,500 kg, is used.) **Every drive starts like a qualifying stop.**

The estimate is kept in the setting `RamWeightGradeState` at qualifying stops, every minute, and when the drive ends.
The kept uncertainty is the learned one, not a widening nothing was learned from since. The next drive starts from it
(widened), but only when it belongs to the same platform and configuration. No VIN, no location is stored.

## Weight floor

The published weight (`massKg`) never goes below the **floor** = empty weight minus the fuel not in the tank (Eric's
decision). Empty weight: the setting `CruiseEmptyWeightLb` when above 0 (Eric's truck: 8,040 lb, its sticker weight),
else Ram's published curb weight for the identified configuration without the options allowance (lower trims may have
fewer options; the lightest candidate when the bed or the 3500 Cummins output is not identified), else no floor. The
fuel not in the tank comes from the fuel gauge (the whole tank when there is no gauge reading), so the floor holds
whether or not the sticker weight includes fuel: Eric's floor is 8,040 lb minus the fuel not in his 31-gallon tank
(at most 219 lb).

The filter itself is not clamped: `massUnclampedKg` is its estimate, `atFloor` says the floor applied and
`massFloorKg` is the floor (0 when there is none), so an estimator error stays visible. The estimate kept between
drives is the unclamped one, with the floor stored next to it for the settings screen.

## Fuel

The fuel gauge (`FUEL_ODO.FUEL_LEVEL`, 0x310, verified in #5): the instrument cluster's own reading as a percentage of a
full tank, in steps of 0.39 percent, about once per second. Eric's decision: read it as it is. Fuel in the tank =
gauge percent / 100 × tank capacity × fuel weight. The tank capacity is the table's for the identified configuration
(31 gallons on Eric's truck; Crew Cab 8 ft trucks of 2020-2024 are assumed to have the 50-gallon tank, see "Starting
weight"); fuel weighs 7.079 lb per US gallon for diesel, 6.25 for gasoline (US Department of Energy, Transportation
Energy Data Book, edition 40, table 6.17).

- **Starting weight and floor**: the fuel not in the tank comes from the gauge (without a gauge, the starting weight
  assumes half a tank and the floor an empty one).
- **Fuel burned** (`fuelBurnedKg`): the gauge's fall while moving. The cluster already damps the gauge, so no extra
  smoothing is needed: the level is the lowest reading so far, which ignores the gauge flickering up by one step now
  and then.
- **Refueling**: at a stop (after 20 seconds), a rise of at least 5 percent that has held within one step for 10
  seconds; the added fuel goes into the weight at once. A rise between drives is found by comparing the first reading
  with the level kept from the last drive.

On Eric's nine drives the gauge fell steadily (13 percent in the 85-minute drive 0000076b) and flickered up by a step
0-9 times per drive.

## Starting weight

Published in `startMassKg`, `startMassStdKg` and `startSource`. In order:

1. **Setting** (`setting`): `CruiseBaseWeightLb` when above 0 (Eric's truck: 8,400 lb with a full tank), minus the
   missing fuel.
2. **Published table** (`table`): Ram's published curb weight for the identified configuration
   + **529 lb options allowance** + **250 lb** (driver and everyday gear) − missing fuel.
   - Options allowance = 8,040 lb (Eric's truck empty, full tank) − 7,511 lb (Ram's published curb weight for Eric's
     configuration: Stellantis 2022 Ram 2500 4x4 weight and tow chart, body builder instruction of 07/07/2021, page 5,
     table "RAM 2500 - 4X4 - CREW CAB - ST - 6'4'' STANDARD BOX"; the base Tradesman's weight, full fuel tank). The same
     529 lb is used for every configuration, RamBox included (Eric's decision).
   - The VIN cannot tell a **RamBox** from the standard box: the standard box's weight is used, and the RamBox's extra
     weight (129 lb on Eric's configuration) widens the uncertainty.
   - On a **3500 with the Cummins** the VIN cannot tell the standard output (68RFE gearbox) from the high output
     (AS69RC): the engine part number decides when the table knows it, otherwise the two are averaged with a wider
     uncertainty.
   - **Bed average** (`tableBedAverage`): the VIN tells the bed (Regular Cab always 8 ft, Mega Cab always 6 ft 4 in,
     Crew Cab codes say which), so this is only for VIN codes missing from the table: the average of both beds, wider.
   - Eric's configuration (2022 Ram 2500 Crew Cab 6 ft 4 in, 4x4, Cummins standard output, 68RFE): 7,511 + 529 + 250
     = **8,290 lb with a full 31-gallon tank**.
3. **Fallback** (`fallback`): the platform's weight from CarParams (3,541 kg, 7,807 lb) with a wide uncertainty
   (600 kg), when the configuration or its weight is unknown.

When the fuel level is not available, the tank is assumed half full, and the uncertainty includes not knowing it.

**Fuel tank size** (Eric's decision): if the truck reports its tank size (a hook is in the mapping table,
`fuel_tank_gal`; not found yet), it is used. Otherwise the table's tank is used, except that **Crew Cab trucks with the
8 ft box of model years 2020-2024 are assumed to have the optional 50-gallon tank** (standard there: 32 gallons; 31 on
6 ft 4 in boxes; 28 on a Regular Cab diesel; owner's manuals' fluid capacities for 2020-2023 and the 2024 media fact
sheet, as listed in the research table). Reason: the optional tank is common on those trucks and the VIN cannot tell.
Then the tank capacity is 50 gallons for the fuel calculations, the extra full-tank fuel (18 gallons × 7.079 lb for
diesel, × 6.25 for gasoline) is added to the published curb weight (which includes a full standard tank), and the
starting uncertainty grows by half that fuel's weight (the chance it is really the 32-gallon tank) and by 30 lb for
the larger tank's own weight, which is not published.

### Configuration from the VIN and part numbers

Only the table's rules are used: no VIN position is known in the code except the model-year character at position 10
(`model_year_codes`). Positions 5-7 are one three-character code (series, rear wheels, drive, cab, bed, and the
Power Wagon / Rebel package); position 8 is the engine ("L" = Cummins of either output). Engine module part numbers
(`CarParams.carFw`, address 0x7E0) add what the table knows (`52370231` = Cummins standard output, 68RFE, 2500: Eric's
truck reports `52370231AK`). The VIN check digit is verified. **The VIN is never logged, stored or published**; the
configuration is published in words in `configuration`.

### The published-weight table

`ram_hd_weights.json` is generated by `make_weight_table.py` from the full research table (about 1 MB, not committed;
retrieved 2026-10-16). That table was built from Stellantis documents: the Ram Heavy Duty weight-and-tow charts and body
builder instructions (curb weight = the published base weight of the base trim with a full fuel tank), owner's
manuals (fuel tanks), media specifications, the 2019 press kit (drag coefficient) and the NHTSA VIN decoding guides.
The slim table keeps per configuration: model year, series, rear wheels, cab, bed, bed length, box variant, package,
drive, engine, transmission, recommended curb weight, its kind, standard and optional fuel tank, and the source id; plus
the sources, fuel weights, VIN rules and engine part numbers. To regenerate:

    python openpilot/sunnypilot/selfdrive/ramweightd/make_weight_table.py <path>/ram_hd_specs.json

## Settings

In **Settings > Vehicle** (the Chrysler/Ram panel), shown only on a Ram Heavy Duty:

- **Starting Weight** (`CruiseBaseWeightLb`): EDIT, type pounds (0 to 40,000). 0 uses Ram's published weight.
- **Empty Weight** (`CruiseEmptyWeightLb`): EDIT, type pounds; the truck's sticker weight (Eric's: 8,040). Sets the
  weight floor. 0 uses Ram's published curb weight for the configuration.
- **Known Extra Load** (`CruiseKnownExtraLb`): 0 to 2,000 lb in 10 lb steps. For a validation drive: the known added
  load (the 250 lb tank, passengers of known weight). **The estimator does not use it** (that would hide its error); it
  is only published as `knownExtraKg`, so that accuracy = estimate − (starting weight + known extra) can be measured.
  Set it back to 0 afterwards.
- **Weight Estimate**: read-only, the kept estimate and its uncertainty in pounds, with the floor applied ("(floor)"
  when it is at the floor).

sunnylink's settings schema (`openpilot/sunnypilot/sunnylink/settings_ui.json`) does not need entries for these: its
tests check only the settings it lists, and nothing requires every backed-up setting to be listed.

## Signals: what is verified, what is not found yet (#5)

All in `RAM_SIGNALS` in `ram_signals.py` (message, signal, bus, status). Verified or known: `ECM_1` ENGINE_RPM and
ENGINE_TORQUE, `ESP_6` wheel speeds, `ESP_8` BRK_PRESSURE and Vehicle_Speed, `ESP_1` Brake_Pedal_State, `ECM_5`
Accelerator_Position, `DAS_3` ACC_ACTIVE and ENGINE_TORQUE_REQUEST (bus 2), `TCM_1` TC_LOCKED, ACTUAL_GEAR,
DESIRED_GEAR, INPUT_SPEED, OUTPUT_SPEED, `ESP_4` Acceleration, `FUEL_ODO` FUEL_LEVEL (the fuel gauge); `ESP_2` TCS_ACTIVE is in the shared database but
unchecked on this truck. Not found yet (hooks, unavailable): exhaust brake, tow/haul, trailer connected, fuel tank size. Seen but not verified, not
used: range to empty 0x30E, fuel rate 0x2A0, odometer in 0x310.

ECM_1 ENGINE_TORQUE is the real engine torque, not an echo of the cruise request: it equals the factory cruise's
request while the cruise holds speed (and differs from it 0.2-10 percent of the time), and the force fit is the same
with the pedal as with the cruise (the acceleration effect gave 8,080 lb with the pedal, 8,230 lb with the cruise).

A signal whose message is not in the database is unavailable and its `ramWeightGrade` field is left out of
`availableSignals`. When #5 verifies more signals, add them to opendbc, fix the message and signal names in
`RAM_SIGNALS` and mark them known; nothing else changes.

`availableSignals` lists `massKg`, `massStdKg`, `dragNPerMps2` once the filter runs, `grade` and `gradeStd` once it has
a grade measurement, and each decoded truck signal (`engineRpm`, `engineTorqueNm`, `gearActual`, `gearDesired`,
`converterLocked`, `shiftPending` (from the two gears), `transInputRpm`, `transOutputRpm`, `serviceBrakeOn`,
`exhaustBrakeOn`, `towHaulOn`, `trailerConnected`, `fuelLevelPct`) only when it comes from a real signal.

## Replay a recorded drive

    # on the Mac (drives copied to ~/src/ram-hd-c3/rlogs/<route>--<n>/rlog.zst)
    python openpilot/sunnypilot/selfdrive/ramweightd/replay.py 00000779--9043655153 --root ~/src/ram-hd-c3/rlogs --known-full-tank-lb 8400
    # on the comma 3
    cd /data/openpilot && PYTHONPATH=/data/openpilot /usr/local/venv/bin/python \
      openpilot/sunnypilot/selfdrive/ramweightd/replay.py 00000779--9043655153 --known-mass-lb 8400

It runs exactly the on-device code over the drive and prints the estimate every minute, the qualifying stops, why
moments were not clean, which grade source and converter rule were used, the estimate kept for the next drive and,
with `--known-mass-lb` (or `--known-full-tank-lb`: the weight with a full tank, minus the fuel the gauge says is
missing), the accuracy. Options: `--base-weight-lb` (as the setting), `--csv FILE` (10 Hz time series),
`--dbc` (another message database), `--efficiency`, `--rolling-resistance`, `--body-pitch` (try constants). The device settings are not read; the VIN is never printed.

## Calibrate the constants

From drives with a **confirmed** combined weight (truck, people, load, fuel at the start; no refueling during a
drive), with varied speeds and grades, strong accelerations and long climbs:

    python openpilot/sunnypilot/selfdrive/ramweightd/calibrate.py <route> [<route> ...] --known-mass-lb 8400 [--root DIR]

It fits efficiency, rolling resistance and the body pitch (which also absorbs rotating-mass errors), plus the air drag
for information, and prints the values to put at the top of `estimator.py`. On Eric's drives, fit with `--no-body-pitch`:
with it, the body-pitch term trades against the grade's remaining noise and gives implausible values. It warns when the drives do not separate
them or the values are not plausible. `--fit-grade-scale` instead checks the grade: how much of the measured grade the
engine force follows (1.00 when the grade measurement is right). Check new constants with `replay.py` on other drives.

## Findings from Eric's drives

Nine drives (291 minutes): 00000776, 00000778, 00000779 (this port, 2026-10-16) and 0000073e, 00000742, 00000748,
0000074a, 0000076a, 0000076b (sunnypilot 0.10.1), replayed read-only. Eric: no drive weighed less than 8,400 lb minus
the fuel not in the tank (a full 31-gallon tank is 219 lb), so every drive was at least about 8,180 lb; some may have
been heavier.

- **The Comma's pitch is too noisy for the grade.** Against ESP_4 and the Comma's own accelerometer (which agree with
  each other), it disagrees by about as much as the real grade varies on mild highway grades; fits against it shrink
  to 0.17-0.33 (regression dilution, reproduced by the simulation in `tests/test_calibrate.py`). Longer averaging (5-30
  seconds) does not help: its errors are slow. On three older drives the logged pitch was nonsense.
- **ESP_4 and the Comma's accelerometer work**: the force followed 0.84-0.91 of their grade (the rest is their noise).
- **Engine torque is real** (see "Signals"), and with the acceleration effect the textbook efficiency 0.85 matches Eric's
  weight; `calibrate.py` fitted 0.850-0.872 on the older drives and 0.852 on the new ones (at 8,180-8,400 lb), rolling
  resistance 0.008-0.009, drag 0.74-0.78. The constants were not changed.
- **Drive 0000076b** (85 minutes, 87 miles) first came out at 7,354 ± 205 lb. Ruled out against the other eight
  drives: a reflash (all module part numbers identical), torque reporting (the fuel-rate candidate 0x2A0 gave 28.0 counts
  per reported kW, like 0000076a's 27.7; torque against EXPECTED_ENGINE_TORQUE, the cruise request and the pedal were
  normal), gear use and converter lock, and top speed alone (0000076a was faster and fine). Cause: with ESP_4 and the
  Comma's accelerometer fused, their shared offset drifted (ESP_4's learned offset went from about 0 to -0.066 m/s²),
  reading every grade about 0.007 too steep; the windows that taught the weight then implied a median of 7,024 lb. The
  estimate stepped down in the first ten minutes and then stayed. Fix: with ESP_4, the Comma's accelerometer is not
  used for the grade.
- Kept estimates now (ESP_4 grade, TC_LOCKED, three-second windows, weight from accelerations, fuel from the gauge),

## Tests

    source .venv/bin/activate && PYTHONPATH=$PWD:$PWD/opendbc_repo python tools/test_runner.py openpilot/sunnypilot/selfdrive/ramweightd/tests/

All on synthetic data (a physics simulation with known weight, drag and grades, noise, signal steps and delays;
synthetic CAN frames; made-up VIN rules and fake VINs; synthetic rlogs replayed end to end). One test compares the
shipped table with a fresh build from the full research table and is skipped where that file is absent.
